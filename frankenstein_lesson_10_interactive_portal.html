How does Shelley build suspense in these chapters?

Exemplar Answer Key:

"Shelley delays revealing who the murder victim is while offering clues that the murderer is the creature and the victim might be Clerval. Key Literary Focus • Chapters 22–23

Dramatic Irony & The Aristotelian Tragic Hero

Analyze Victor Frankenstein as a classic tragic hero whose fatal flaw (hamartia), self-absorbed hubris, and anagnorisis catalyze the wedding-night catastrophe.

AP Literature Master Concept Tragic Structure & Cosmic Irony

The Fatal Solipsism: Victor as Tragic Hero

Aristotelian tragedy posits that a protagonist of noble birth and lofty aspirations falls not through inherent wickedness, but through an error in judgment or character blind spot (hamartia). In Chapters 22–23, Shelley deploys Dramatic Irony at its highest pitch: while readers and the Creature recognize that companionship is the monster's only grievance, Victor's consuming hubris convinces him that he alone is the intended victim. By locking Elizabeth inside the bridal suite to protect her while he prowls the perimeter, Victor's blindness directly orchestrates her murder.

Device 1 • Structural Tension

Agonizing Dramatic Irony

Narrative Split

The reader knows the Creature’s entire crusade stems from the denial of a bride. When the Creature vows, "I shall be with you on your wedding-night," the audience anticipates Elizabeth's peril. Victor's inability to see what is glaringly obvious to everyone else creates unbearable narrative dread.

"I had choked every sensation of humanity... and imagined that the monster had no other purpose than my own destruction."
Device 2 • Character Flaw

Hamartia & Egotism

Tragic Defect

Victor's hamartia is his unrelenting solipsism. Even during romance and wedding preparations, Victor interprets every cosmic event through the prism of his own martyrdom. He assumes the creature’s wrath exists purely to duel *him*, making Elizabeth an unwitting casualty of his self-absorption.

"Great God! Why did I not then expire! Why am I here to relate the destruction of the best that ever lived?"
Device 3 • The Reversal

Anagnorisis & Peripeteia

Climactic Shift

The scream from the bridal room produces instantaneous anagnorisis (tragic recognition). In that moment, Victor's illusions collapse; he reaches the "acme of horrors." His defensive posture instantly shifts into irreversible loss, transforming him from a passive prey into an engine of pure vengeance.

"The arms had ceased to drop down... A grin was on the face of the monster; he seemed to jeer, as with his fiendish finger he pointed towards the corpse."

The Anatomy of Victor's Downfall (Aristotelian Tragedy Matrix)

AP Lit Question 12 Analysis
1. High Stature & Noble Aspirations

Born of a distinguished Genevan magistracy line; intended his scientific discoveries to banish disease and conquer mortality for the welfare of mankind.

2. Hubris & Transgression

Usurped natural reproduction and divine prerogative, proudly aspiring to omnipotence while neglecting the moral duties owed to sentient life.

3. Hamartia (Fatal Blind Spot)

Pervasive solipsism. Because he views himself as the hero/martyr of the universe, he cannot fathom that the Creature's retribution would target Elizabeth.

4. Catastrophic Anagnorisis & Exile

The scream in Chapter 23 strips away all delusion. With Elizabeth and Alphonse dead, he becomes the isolated wanderer he forced the Creature to be.

Interactive Analysis Task

Critical Reflection: How does Victor's rebuke to the Genevan magistrate—“How ignorant art thou in thy pride of wisdom”—epitomize the ultimate tragic irony of his character?

AP Literature Exemplar Key:

The statement is steeped in supreme psychological projection and dramatic irony. Victor condemns the magistrate for relying on rational skepticism and the conventional limits of human knowledge, scorning his "pride of wisdom." Yet it was Victor’s own arrogant "pride of wisdom"—his hubristic quest to penetrate the secrets of life and death without foreseeing the consequences—that brought the monster into existence and doomed his entire domestic world. In rebuking the magistrate, Victor unconsciously passes judgment on himself.
